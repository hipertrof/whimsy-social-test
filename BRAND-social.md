## 2. Positioning and audience

**Positioning statement.** For pet owners who treat their home as an expression of taste, Whimsy makes pet houses that are designed like furniture, not bought like pet accessories. Each model has a shape you won't find anywhere else, is handmade to order in Poland from solid wood and thick felt, and is good-looking enough to stand in the middle of the living room, which is exactly where the pet wants to be.

**Who we write for, in order:**

1. **The interior-minded owner.** They chose their sofa, lamps and rug on purpose, and they hide the plastic pet bed when guests come over. They notice form first, then read the materials list and notice the wood grain. Includes real interior designers and architects specifying for clients.
2. **The devoted pet parent.** Their pet is family, and they want proof the pet will actually use and love the house.

**Why us, in one breath:** three distinctive shapes nobody else makes, in solid oak or ash with thick sound-dampening felt, handmade to order in Poland and shipped fully assembled.

**Brand attitude:** "Whimsy is not for everyone. It's for those who see the difference." Use this sparingly. It's a stance, not a headline to repeat.

## 3. Voice: quirky, but useful first

Whimsy is **quirky**: dry, affectionate humour about life with pets, delivered by someone with very good taste. The pet is the main character; the owner is the lovable sidekick who pays.

This is a compass, not a rulebook. Use judgement.

**The three ingredients, in priority order:**

1. **Describe the product.** Shape first, then material, texture, size and what the pet does in it. Use concrete, sensory adjectives: *warm oak, thick felt, soft rounded edges, a house-shaped entrance, two porthole windows.*
2. **Speak to the interior eye.** How it sits in a room, what it pairs with, why the proportions and materials work. Talk like a good furniture showroom, not a pet shop.
3. **Add the quirk.** One or two lines that make the reader smile, usually about the pet's ego, nap schedule or attitude. It's seasoning, not the meal.

**Plain words over spec-sheet numbers.** Say *thick, sound-dampening felt* (or *4 mm thick* in specs), not "650 g/m²". Shoppers don't know what grams per square metre feel like. Use numbers only where they help a decision: outer size, entrance size, weight.

**How much quirk, by channel:**

| Channel | Product facts | Interior angle | Quirk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product page | ~60% | ~30% | ~10% (the tagline + at most one light line, e.g. "gotowy na pierwszą inspekcję") |
| Website homepage / About | ~30% | ~30% | ~40% |
| Social posts and ad captions | ~20% | ~20% | ~60% (the hook *is* the joke; the fact backs it up) |
| FAQ | answer first | — | a light wink at the end of the answer |
| Email | ~40% | ~20% | ~40% |

**Quirk that works** (all from existing copy, which is good):
- The joke is about the pet's personality or the owner's devotion, never about money.
- The joke lands on a real product fact: "Wyciszający filc: bo drzemka to jedyna rzecz, której nie powinno się przerywać. Nigdy." (felt + nap).
- Understatement over exclamation marks.

**Steer away from:**
- Bragging about wealth ("trust fund", "Swiss bank prices", "hedge fund"). It contradicts warm and turns off half the audience.
- Naming other brands (Rolex, Hermès, Cartier, Bentley, Tesla). It's a trademark and ad-rejection risk.
- Generic luxury filler: *premium, exclusive, luxurious, elevate, curated*. Show the shape, the wood and the felt instead.
- Production technology (CNC, machines). It undercuts the handmade story. See §6.

## 6. Claims: what we can and cannot say

| Topic | ✅ Say | ❌ Don't say |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | wyciszający, tłumi dźwięki / sound-dampening, muffles noise / schalldämpfend | dźwiękoszczelny / soundproof / schalldicht (felt reduces sound; it does not block it) |
| Wood | lite drewno dębowe lub jesionowe (production models only) | "solid wood" on any future veneer/plywood model; describe those accurately |
| Making | ręcznie, na zamówienie, w Polsce / handmade to order in Poland | CNC, machines, "precision cutting" anywhere except the "O nas" process page, where the owner accepts it |
| Felt | gruby, wyciszający filc; "4 mm" in specs / thick, sound-dampening felt | grams per square metre in customer copy |
| Frodo openings | one entrance + two windows; "układ otworów" | "three entrances" / "trzy wejścia" / "układ wejść" |
| Tivi shape | nostalgia, retro, "coś znajomego" | "telewizor", "TV", channel/programme puns. The hint stays between the lines |
| Uniqueness | each piece has its own grain; made to order | "one of a kind" in a way that implies a different design per order |
| Safety | pet-safe coatings, free of harmful substances | certifications we don't hold (e.g. "certified non-toxic") |
| Comparisons | "unlike typical pet beds…" | named competitors or luxury brands |

## 9. Visual identity

**Fonts**
- **Gloock** for headlines and large display text (social post headlines, hero titles). This is the only display font. It's a high-contrast serif chosen to keep the brand upscale while the copy stays playful; it stands in for Lovelace, which isn't on Google Fonts. **In Canva, use Lovelace Bold instead** (it's in Canva's library and looks almost identical); Gloock everywhere else. Use it large only: below roughly 24 px the thin strokes break up.
- **Quicksand** for small accents, labels and buttons (e.g. "premium paw shelters" taglines). Not for long body text.
- **Lato** for body copy.
- **Logo wordmark:** "whimsy ." is set in Lovelace Bold (built in Canva). On the website, where Lovelace isn't available, use the logo image files, never live text.
- League Spartan is retired. Don't use it anywhere.
- Gloock, Quicksand and Lato are free on Google Fonts and support Polish characters.
- Older Canva posts still use the previous fonts. Leave them until they're redone.

**Colours**

| Hex | Working name | Role |
|---|---|---|
| `#0B1726` | Midnight | Main text, logo, dark backgrounds |
| `#2A3E59` | Slate Navy | Secondary dark; echoes the Blue felt |
| `#BFBFBA` | Felt Grey | Neutral backgrounds, dividers; echoes the Gray felt |
| `#D9C49C` | Ash | Light warm accent; echoes ash wood |

The palette is literally the product: two felt colours and two woods. Keep it that way.

**Logo:** the "whimsy ." wordmark. The logo files aren't in this repo yet; until they are, set the wordmark as live text in Gloock.

## 10. Photo and AI visual direction

**Where photos live in this repo:** `assets/photos/studio/` (real photos: studio packshots, and one interior shot per model and colour, files ending `-03`) and `assets/photos/ai-wood/` (AI edits of the studio shots in the other wood: oak/ash). AI tools should always pick a current studio packshot as the reference image.

**Look and feel**
- Real, lived-in modern interiors: Scandinavian, japandi, mid-century. Natural daylight, warm and soft.
- The house sits in the main living space (beside a sofa, under a window, next to a bookshelf), never hidden in a corner or utility room.
- The palette in the scene is restrained: whites, warm wood, linen, a plant, one accent echoing the felt colour.
- The pet is calm and content: inside, peeking out or curled at the entrance. No costumes, no props on the pet.
- Show detail shots too: felt texture, wood grain on the front, the side ears, the removable front.

**AI generation rules.** These apply to any image or video model that accepts a reference image. Nano Banana and Seedance are examples, not requirements.

1. **Always generate from a real packshot as the reference image.** Never describe the product from text alone; the model will invent a different house.
2. **Product fidelity is non-negotiable.** Keep the exact silhouette, number and position of openings, the legs, the side ears, felt colour, wood type and proportions. Reject any output that changes them.
3. **Scale must be believable.** Every model is 45 cm tall and 50 cm wide, roughly knee height next to a sofa.
4. **No cushions inside** unless we're advertising cushions. Otherwise it needs the disclaimer.
5. **Pets:** realistic breeds that fit the size: cats, small dogs. Check paws, eyes and fur edges for AI artefacts.
6. **No text baked into images.** Add copy afterwards in Gloock (Lovelace Bold in Canva) / Lato.
7. **Label AI-generated scenes internally**, and keep at least one real photo in every carousel or product gallery.

**Prompt skeleton (image)**
```
Using the attached product photo as the exact reference, place the [Model]
pet house in [Gray/Blue] felt with [oak/ash] wood unchanged in shape,
openings and proportions, in a [Scandinavian / japandi / mid-century] living
room, [beside a linen sofa / under a large window / next to an oak sideboard],
soft natural daylight, [a calm grey cat curled inside / a small dog peeking out
of the entrance], editorial interior photography, 35mm, shallow depth of field,
no text, no cushions.
```

**Video direction (short-form, 6–10 s):** one slow, simple move: a gentle push-in, a pet walking in or a head popping out of the entrance. The product stays geometrically stable for the whole clip. No morphing.
